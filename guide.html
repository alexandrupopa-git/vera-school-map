How to read Vera's School Map

One home from autumn 2028: state Reception in Sept 2029 and Year 7 in Sept 2036. Chances are history, not promises. Open the map · Sources

1 · Where to look

The green hex surface scores every point: good primaries and strong secondaries Vera would realistically get into from there (High = 2 / 3 points, Medium = 1 / 1.5, grammar edge +0.5; weights on the Map tab). Darker is better. Grey hexes fail a requirement you set — e.g. "2 good primaries High + 1 strong secondary High + rent ≤ £2,500 + ≤ 40 min to Waterloo".

Numbered black pins are the workbook's 11 ranked spots; the blue outlines are their search zones: streets from which every High-rated key school stays inside its comfort radius. Hand the zone's postcode sectors and streets to agents (Pin tab → Search zone), or open the zone drawn directly on Rightmove or Zoopla from the same place — results then come only from inside the shape.

2 · Check any address

Tap the map, or type a postcode or "lat, lon" and press Check this spot. The Pin tab answers: verdict, counts, every school in reach with its chance and reason, rent (now and 2028), commute and monthly total, zone, listing links, daily life, risks. Drag a pin to move it. Save keeps it with notes; + Compare puts two or three side by side; Share view copies a link that reopens everything.

On a viewing trip: open the hosted link on your phone and press ⌖ Me.

3 · The chance labels

  • High inside 75 % (Reception) / 85 % (Year 7) of the tightest 2024–26 cut-off, or every applicant was offered
  • Medium inside the tightest cut-off, not the margin; also lotteries and ability-banded intakes
  • Low inside only the widest recent cut-off
  • Very low outside every recent cut-off
  • Test-dependent grammar and test places — a bonus, never the plan
  • Unknown no usable data (e.g. Herts does not publish Sandringham's distance)
  • Faith decides shown, never counted

Rule-based schools (nearest-school in Herts, feeder primaries, Waldegrave's areas, ballots, bands) explain themselves on their row. "Borderline" means within 8 % of the next threshold. The stress-test slider shrinks every cut-off to see which spots survive.

4 · Quality bars and labels

Good primary: Ofsted Good/Outstanding and 2-year KS2 reading-writing-maths ≥ 61.5 % (England). Strong secondary: Attainment 8 ≥ 51.2 (England 46.2 + 5). Both are sliders. Every secondary row says comprehensive / grammar / partially selective, banded or aptitude places, girls or mixed, and faith.

5 · Rings and distance

Click a school: dotted 2023, dashed 2024/25, solid 2026, tinted disc = comfort radius. Rings are straight lines from the school's postcode centre (±100–200 m). Richmond and Hounslow measure the walking route: the panel estimates it (straight line × 1.25, via the nearest bridge where the line crosses the Thames) and fetches a live route when online. The workbooks used the straight line, so a few of their ratings differ by one step; both are shown.

6 · Money and commute

Rent = the nearest researched postcode sector's Hutch average and listing samples (late 2026), coloured against £2,500 / £3,000; the 2028 figure compounds the borough's ONS annual change for two years and is a projection. Commute = walk to the nearest station + 5 min + TfL's peak journey time (arrive by 08:45) + 10 min at the other end; monthly ticket from TfL March 2026 fares or operator seasons.

What is not on the map

  • Flood zones, Defra noise, air quality, crime, deprivation and census layers — each is a one-click link to the official map at your pin instead.
  • Isochrones; Merton, Waldegrave, Stamford Green and Herts priority-area boundaries (flagged per school with the official map link).
  • Heathrow corridors are approximate, Claude-drawn; check WebTrak.
  • Rightmove result pages cannot be deep-linked from a postcode, so its link opens the search form prefilled — one more click.
